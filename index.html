Prednosti konzumiranja orahaBenefits of eating walnuts

Orah je jedna od nutritivno najbogatijih namirnica koje možete imati u kuhinji. Već jedna šaka dnevno (oko 30 g) donosi zdrave masti, biljne proteine, vlakna i niz minerala. Evo šta orahe izdvaja:

Walnuts are one of the most nutrient-dense foods you can keep in your kitchen. A single handful a day (about 30 g) provides healthy fats, plant protein, fibre and a range of minerals. Here's what makes walnuts stand out:

Zdravlje srca i krvnih sudovaHeart and blood vessels

Evropska agencija za bezbednost hrane (EFSA) potvrdila je da orasi doprinose poboljšanju elastičnosti krvnih sudova, uz dnevni unos od 30 g. Većina masti u orasima su nezasićene masti, koje su bolji izbor od zasićenih.

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) has confirmed that walnuts contribute to the improvement of the elasticity of blood vessels, with a daily intake of 30 g. Most of the fat in walnuts is unsaturated, which is a better choice than saturated fat.

Biljni izvor omega-3Plant-based omega-3

Orah je jedini orašasti plod sa značajnom količinom alfa-linolenske kiseline (ALA), biljne omega-3 masne kiseline: oko 2,7 g u 30 g. ALA doprinosi održavanju normalnog nivoa holesterola u krvi, uz dnevni unos od 2 g.

Walnuts are the only tree nut with a significant amount of alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), a plant omega-3 fatty acid: about 2.7 g per 30 g. ALA contributes to the maintenance of normal blood cholesterol levels, with a daily intake of 2 g.

AntioksidansiAntioxidants

Orasi sadrže polifenole (posebno elagitanine, uglavnom u tankoj opni oko jezgra), gama-tokoferol (oblik vitamina E) i bakar i mangan, koji doprinose zaštiti ćelija od oksidativnog stresa.

Walnuts contain polyphenols (especially ellagitannins, mostly in the thin skin around the kernel), gamma-tocopherol (a form of vitamin E), and copper and manganese, which contribute to the protection of cells from oxidative stress.

Minerali za svaki danEveryday minerals

Šaka oraha pokriva oko polovine dnevnih potreba za bakrom i manganom. Bakar doprinosi normalnom funkcionisanju imunog sistema, magnezijum smanjenju umora i iscrpljenosti, a mangan održavanju normalnih kostiju.

A handful of walnuts covers about half of your daily copper and manganese needs. Copper contributes to the normal function of the immune system, magnesium to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue, and manganese to the maintenance of normal bones.

Vlakna i sitostFibre and fullness

Kombinacija masti, proteina i vlakana čini orahe zasitnom užinom. Umesto keksa ili čipsa, šaka oraha daje dugotrajniju energiju bez dodatog šećera i soli.

The combination of fat, protein and fibre makes walnuts a filling snack. Instead of biscuits or crisps, a handful of walnuts gives longer-lasting energy with no added sugar or salt.

Tema brojnih istraživanjaA subject of ongoing research

Naučnici ispituju vezu između redovnog jedenja oraha i zdravlja mozga, crevne mikroflore i kontrole telesne mase. Rezultati su obećavajući, ali još nisu konačni, pa ih navodimo kao zanimljivost, a ne kao tvrdnju.

Scientists are studying links between eating walnuts regularly and brain health, gut microbiota and weight management. Results are promising but not yet conclusive, so we mention them as a point of interest rather than a claim.

A traditional Serbian festive cookie with no flour, made from just a few ingredients.

Ingredients

  • 300 g ground walnuts
  • 100 g finely chopped walnuts (for coating)
  • 250 g sugar
  • 3 egg whites
  • grated zest of one lemon
  • icing sugar for dusting (optional)

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 160 °C and line a baking tray with baking paper.
  2. Whisk the egg whites with the sugar into a thick foam.
  3. Gently fold in the ground walnuts and lemon zest. The mixture should be sticky but mouldable.
  4. With wet hands, shape small crescents, roll them in the chopped walnuts and place them on the tray.
  5. Bake for 15 to 20 minutes until light golden. Let them cool completely on the tray before lifting them off.

A home-style, more affordable take on classic pesto, with walnuts in place of pine nuts. Great with pasta, on bread or with roasted vegetables.

Ingredients

  • 60 g walnuts (lightly toasted)
  • 50 g fresh basil
  • 40 g grated hard cheese (Parmesan or a mature cow's cheese)
  • 1 to 2 garlic cloves
  • 100 ml olive oil
  • juice of half a lemon, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Pulse the walnuts and garlic briefly in a blender or food processor.
  2. Add the basil, cheese and lemon juice, then blend into a thick paste while slowly pouring in the oil.
  3. Season with salt and pepper to taste.
  4. Keep in a jar in the fridge for up to 5 days, covered with a thin layer of oil.

A crunchy breakfast with no industrial additives. Serve with yoghurt, milk or fresh fruit.

Ingredients

  • 250 g rolled oats
  • 120 g roughly chopped walnuts
  • 50 g sunflower or pumpkin seeds
  • 4 tbsp honey
  • 3 tbsp oil (sunflower or coconut)
  • 1 tsp cinnamon and a pinch of salt
  • 80 g raisins or chopped prunes

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 160 °C.
  2. Gently warm the honey and oil until combined, then add the cinnamon and salt.
  3. In a large bowl mix the oats, walnuts and seeds, pour over the honey mixture and stir well.
  4. Spread thinly on a tray lined with baking paper and bake for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring halfway, until golden.
  5. Once cool, stir in the dried fruit. Keep in a closed jar for up to two weeks.

A quick sweet snack and a lovely homemade gift in a jar. Also great over ice cream or porridge.

Ingredients

  • 200 g walnut halves
  • 3 tbsp sugar (or 2 tbsp honey)
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 1/2 tsp cinnamon and a pinch of salt

Method

  1. Melt the butter with the sugar and cinnamon in a pan over medium heat.
  2. Add the walnuts and stir constantly for 4 to 5 minutes, until the sugar caramelises and coats them.
  3. Tip them straight onto baking paper, separate them with a fork and sprinkle with a pinch of salt.
  4. Once cooled and hardened, keep them in a closed jar.

Careful: caramel is very hot, don't taste the walnuts until they have cooled.

Between meals, we usually grab whatever is closest: a biscuit, a pastry or a chocolate bar. These snacks give a quick boost, but it fades just as fast, and an hour later we're hungry again.

Walnuts work differently. A handful (about 30 g) contains healthy fats, about 4.6 g of protein and 2 g of fibre. This combination is digested more slowly, so you stay full for longer, with no added sugar or salt.

Walnuts are practical too: they don't need a fridge, they don't crumble in your bag and they need no preparation. Put them in a small box and take them to work, school or on a trip.

How to fit them into your day

  • Morning: sprinkle them over porridge or yoghurt.
  • At work: a handful of walnuts and an apple make a great snack.
  • After a workout: walnuts with a little dried fruit restore your energy.
  • Evening: instead of crisps with a film, try the caramelised walnuts from our recipes.

The only rule is moderation: walnuts are energy-dense, so one handful a day is plenty.

Proleće: cvetanje i zametanje plodova

Orah cveta u aprilu i maju. Na istom stablu nalaze se i muški cvetovi (rese) i ženski cvetovi, a oprašivanje obavlja vetar. Zato je prolećni mraz najveća briga: jedna hladna noć u vreme cvetanja može da odnese veliki deo roda.

Leto: rast i nega

Tokom leta plod raste, a ljuska se postepeno stvrdnjava. To je period kada voćnjak treba pratiti najpažljivije: navodnjavanje u sušnim nedeljama, praćenje bolesti i štetočina i održavanje međurednog prostora.

Jesen: berba

Berba počinje krajem septembra i traje kroz oktobar. Znak da je orah zreo je zelena spoljašnja ljuska koja počinje da puca i odvaja se od ploda. Što se orah brže očisti od te ljuske, to je jezgro svetlije i ukusnije.

Sušenje i čuvanje

Sveže obran orah ima previše vlage za dugo čuvanje. Zato se pere i polako suši na vazduhu, dok vlažnost jezgra ne padne na oko 8%. Prebrzo sušenje na visokoj temperaturi može da pokvari ukus, pa je strpljenje ovde ključno.

Sortiranje i pakovanje

Na kraju se orasi sortiraju, odvajaju se oštećeni i prazni plodovi, a deo se krcka i ručno čisti u jezgra. Tek tada su spremni da krenu ka vama.

The walnut in your hand took almost a whole year to grow. Here's what it goes through before it reaches you.

Spring: flowering and fruit set

Walnut trees flower in April and May. The same tree carries both male flowers (catkins) and female flowers, and pollination is done by the wind. That's why spring frost is the biggest worry: one cold night during flowering can take a large part of the crop.

Summer: growth and care

Over the summer the fruit grows and the shell gradually hardens. This is when the orchard needs the closest attention: irrigation in dry weeks, monitoring for diseases and pests, and keeping the rows tidy.

Autumn: harvest

Harvest starts at the end of September and runs through October. The sign of a ripe walnut is the green outer husk starting to split and come away from the nut. The sooner the husk is removed, the lighter and tastier the kernel.

Drying and storage

Freshly picked walnuts contain too much moisture to keep for long. They're washed and dried slowly in the air until the kernel moisture falls to around 8%. Drying too fast at high heat can spoil the flavour, so patience is key.

Sorting and packing

Finally the walnuts are sorted, damaged and empty nuts are removed, and some are cracked and hand-shelled into kernels. Only then are they ready to head your way.

Glavni neprijatelji oraha

  • Bakteriozna plamenjača oraha (Xanthomonas arboricola pv. juglandis) najopasnija je bolest u našim uslovima. Napada lišće, cvetove i mlade plodove, na kojima se javljaju crne mrlje, a zaraženi plodovi često opadaju. Posebno je jaka u kišnim i toplim prolećima.
  • Mrka pegavost lista (antraknoza, Ophiognomonia leptostyla) je gljivična bolest koja izaziva mrke pege na listovima i plodovima, slabi stablo i smanjuje kvalitet jezgra.
  • Orahova muva (Rhagoletis completa) je relativno nova štetočina u Srbiji. Larve se hrane zelenom ljuskom, koja zato pocrni i zalepi se za orah, pa jezgro potamni i teško se čisti.
  • Jabukin smotavac (Cydia pomonella) napada i orah: larva ulazi u plod i oštećuje jezgro.

Kako se voćnjak štiti

Zaštita počinje od dobrih agrotehničkih mera: pravilnog razmaka sadnje, rezidbe koja omogućava provetravanje krošnje, uklanjanja opalog lišća i zaraženih plodova. Za praćenje štetočina koriste se žute lepljive i feromonske klopke, a tretmani se rade kada su zaista potrebni, prema preporukama prognozno-izveštajne službe zaštite bilja.

Koriste se samo registrovana sredstva za zaštitu bilja, uz poštovanje karence, odnosno propisanog vremena između poslednjeg tretmana i berbe. Tako plod koji stigne do potrošača ostaje bezbedan.

Zašto je to važno vama

Kada kupujete orah, kupujete i ceo taj trud. Svetlo, puno i zdravo jezgro nije slučajnost, već rezultat nege koja traje od ranog proleća do berbe.

Walnuts were long believed to be trees that "grow on their own". In a backyard, where a tree stands alone, that's often true. But in an orchard, where trees are planted closely and regular, high-quality yields are expected, the picture is completely different.

The walnut's main enemies

  • Walnut bacterial blight (Xanthomonas arboricola pv. juglandis) is the most dangerous disease in our conditions. It attacks leaves, flowers and young fruit, causing black spots, and infected nuts often drop. It is especially severe in warm, rainy springs.
  • Walnut anthracnose (Ophiognomonia leptostyla) is a fungal disease that causes brown spots on leaves and fruit, weakens the tree and lowers kernel quality.
  • Walnut husk fly (Rhagoletis completa) is a relatively new pest in Serbia. Its larvae feed on the green husk, which turns black and sticks to the nut, darkening the kernel and making it hard to clean.
  • Codling moth (Cydia pomonella) also attacks walnuts: the larva bores into the nut and damages the kernel.

How the orchard is protected

Protection starts with good growing practice: proper tree spacing, pruning that lets air move through the canopy, and removing fallen leaves and infected fruit. Yellow sticky traps and pheromone traps are used to monitor pests, and treatments are applied only when they are really needed, following the advice of the regional plant protection forecasting service.

Only registered plant protection products are used, respecting the pre-harvest interval, the required time between the last treatment and harvest. That keeps the nuts that reach consumers safe.

Why this matters to you

When you buy walnuts, you're buying all of that effort. A light, full and healthy kernel is no accident; it's the result of care that lasts from early spring to harvest.

Očišćena jezgra

  • U ostavi: u zatvorenoj tegli, nekoliko nedelja.
  • U frižideru: u dobro zatvorenoj posudi, oko šest meseci.

Kako da prepoznate užegao orah

Svež orah miriše blago i slatkasto. Ako jezgro miriše na boju ili staro ulje, ako je oštro gorko ili lepljivo, nemojte ga jesti.

Walnuts are rich in unsaturated fats, and that's exactly what makes them delicate. Heat, light and air speed up fat oxidation, so a walnut left too long somewhere warm develops a bitter, rancid taste.

In-shell walnuts

The shell is the best natural protection. Keep in-shell walnuts in a cool, dry, dark place, in a cloth sack or a mesh bag that lets air through. Stored like this, they can last up to a year.

Shelled kernels

  • In the pantry: in a closed jar, for a few weeks.
  • In the fridge: in a well-sealed container, for about six months.
  • In the freezer: in a freezer bag, for up to a year. There's no need to thaw them; use them straight away.

Walnuts easily absorb odours, so don't keep them next to onions, spices or fish.

How to spot a rancid walnut

A fresh walnut smells mild and slightly sweet. If the kernel smells of paint or old oil, tastes sharply bitter or feels sticky, don't eat it.

At the market or in a shop, walnuts often look the same, but the difference in quality can be big. Here's what to look out for.

In-shell walnuts

  • Weight: a good walnut feels heavy for its size. A light nut often hides a dry or empty kernel.
  • Shell: it should be clean, with no dark stains, cracks or traces of mould.
  • Sound: when you shake the nut, the kernel shouldn't rattle. Rattling means the kernel has dried out and shrunk.

Shelled kernels

  • Colour: a light, even colour, from straw to light brown, signals freshness and a good harvest. Darker kernels aren't necessarily spoiled, but they usually taste weaker.
  • Appearance: kernels should be plump and firm, not shrivelled.
  • Smell and taste: mild, nutty and slightly sweet. A rancid smell means the walnut is old or badly stored.

Ask about the harvest

The simplest tip is to ask which harvest the walnuts are from. With a grower who sells directly, that's always easy to find out, and walnuts from the latest autumn are always the best choice.

When people talk about walnuts, the amount most often mentioned is 30 grams a day. That's the amount behind the official EU health claim on blood vessel elasticity, and it also covers the recommended daily intake of 2 g of plant omega-3.

What 30 grams looks like

  • one full handful of shelled kernels,
  • about 7 whole walnuts, or about 14 halves,
  • about 3 heaped tablespoons of chopped walnuts.

Why not more?

Walnuts are healthy but energy-dense: 30 g has about 196 kcal. It's best for walnuts to replace another snack rather than be added on top of everything else you eat during the day.

Things to keep in mind

  • Walnuts are a common allergen. Take extra care with young children and anyone allergic to tree nuts.
  • Don't give whole walnuts to children under five because of the choking risk; grind them instead.
  • If you follow a special diet or have a health condition, check with a doctor or nutritionist.

In short: a handful a day, ideally raw and unsalted, is a simple and tasty addition to a varied diet.
